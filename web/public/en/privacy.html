Privacy policy

Effective: October 29, 2026

This is a translation for convenience. If it differs from the Chinese version, the Chinese version governs.

This policy explains what personal information the still.fail hosted service collects, how it's used, who it's shared with, how long it's kept, and what rights you have. Please read it carefully, especially the parts in bold.

1. Who processes your information

still.fail is run by an individual developer based in mainland China (“we”). For any questions or requests about personal information, email hi@still.fail.

2. Which services this policy covers

This policy applies to the still.fail hosted service we operate, including the still.fail website, the web app (app.still.fail), public share pages (share.still.fail), preview pages (preview.still.fail), and the desktop and Android apps when they connect to these services (together, the “Service”). It also applies to the beta channel youdid.wtf.

This policy does not apply to:

  • Stations on your own machines: a station runs on a computer or server belonging to you (or to a member of your workspace). Chat history, files and the agents' work are stored there, under the control of you and your workspace, and we can't access them;
  • Services that others deploy themselves from the still.fail open-source code;
  • Third-party services you use on your stations, such as Claude, ChatGPT/Codex, Hermes Agent and the model services you configure for it, Slack and GitHub. Their own privacy policies apply.

3. The most important point first: your chat content isn't stored with us

still.fail's agents run on your own stations. Your devices connect directly to your stations with end-to-end encryption; when a direct connection isn't possible, traffic is forwarded through a relay server, which only passes on encrypted data and can't see the content. Our servers don't store your chat content, attachments or files.

There are two exceptions, where content passes through or is stored with us:

  • Push notifications: to show notifications on your devices, your station sends the chat title, the sender's name and the first 140 characters of the message through our servers to Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging (Android), Apple Push Notification service (iOS) or your browser's push service (web). We only forward this text; we don't store it. On a device where you turn off “Show content in notifications”, its notifications only say what happened (such as “New message”), and the message's text is not forwarded;
  • Issue reports: issue reports that you or the agents on your station choose to submit (title, description, context and logs) are stored on our servers so we can investigate.

Publicly shared chats are also stored on the publisher's station; visitors' browsers read them directly from the station over an encrypted connection. The key after the # in a share link is never sent to our servers.

When you use shared debugging, the station keeps the time the chat last actually used debugging while its grant is active and sends it to your devices over the encrypted connection, to display and sort the list. When the grant ends, that time is removed from the grant record. Recording that time does not store the contents of debugging commands.

4. What we collect

  • Account information: if you sign in with Google, your Google account ID, email address, name and avatar URL; if you sign in with an email code, your email address. We also keep when your account was created and last active, and its plan, eligibility and ban status;
  • Sign-in sessions and devices: device or client names (such as a computer's name, or a phone's as set in its settings), when sessions were created and when they expire, and when devices were first and last online;
  • Workspace information: workspace names, members and roles, invitations (including invitees' email addresses), invite codes, and the names, icons, versions and online times of stations that have joined;
  • Push tokens: the tokens or push endpoints your devices or browsers register to receive notifications, with that device's notification settings (its language, which kinds of notifications it shows, whether it shows their content);
  • Issue reports: see section 3;
  • Network and log information: the IP address, request path, time and browser information (User-Agent) when you access the Service, recorded in Cloudflare's request logs; relay servers can see connections' IP addresses, node public keys and traffic volume. To prevent abuse, we rate-limit sign-ins using salted hashes of IP addresses and email addresses;
  • Usage statistics and error information: the web app and the Android app send PostHog action events, counts, durations, program errors and stack traces, and the web app also records session replays (all text, input and images on the page are masked at recording time). This data is tied to your account ID only, without your email address or name. You can turn off “Send usage and error reports” in the app's settings; once it's off, that device sends nothing to PostHog. Clients, stations and servers also send request traces to Axiom, including account ID, session ID, request path and duration, and the way each connection goes, its round trip, and how long the app was off screen before it came back. These statistics and traces don't include chat text, file names, prompts, agent output or conversation titles. Traces are used to diagnose failures and performance problems and aren't affected by that switch. Stations also send their run logs to Axiom by default (what the station did and when: its connections to still.fail and to other stations, errors, versions), with home directory paths, email addresses, things that look like keys or tokens, quoted text and fields that may hold what someone wrote removed first; they don't include chat text or file contents. The workspace's admins can turn this off with “Send run logs to still.fail” under Diagnostics in the station's settings in the app, or in the station's config.json ("telemetry": { "logs": false }). Station error reports to PostHog are sent only if the station's user turns them on; they're off by default.

The still.fail website (still.fail) itself doesn't use analytics.

5. How we use it

  • To sign you in, identify your account, devices and workspaces, and let your devices find and connect to your stations;
  • To send sign-in codes and notifications;
  • To enforce plan limits, prevent abuse and attacks, and keep accounts secure;
  • To troubleshoot problems, understand how features are used, and improve the Service;
  • To respond to your questions and requests, and to meet legal obligations.

We don't sell your personal information, use it for advertising, or use your data to train AI models.

6. Cookies and local storage

  • Signing in uses a cookie valid for 10 minutes, to complete sign-in securely;
  • The web app stores sign-in credentials and app data in your browser's IndexedDB, and interface preferences (theme, sidebar width and so on) in localStorage;
  • PostHog analytics in the web app stores its own identifier in your browser, and whether you turned statistics off.

We don't use third-party advertising cookies.

8. Cross-border transfer of personal information

Because Cloudflare, Google, PostHog and Axiom in the table above are located outside mainland China, your account information, device and workspace information, push notification text, usage statistics, error information and logs are transferred outside mainland China (mainly to the United States) to be stored and processed for the purposes listed in section 5. You can exercise your rights against us or these recipients as described in section 10. Contact details for the overseas recipients are on their websites. By signing in to and using the Service, you consent to these cross-border transfers; if you don't agree, don't use the Service.

9. How long we keep it

  • Sign-in codes: 10 minutes, deleted once used;
  • Sign-in sessions: up to 30 days; they expire after 7 days without use;
  • Invitations: 7 days;
  • Push tokens: deleted when you sign out or the device stops receiving notifications;
  • Account, workspace and station information: kept while you use the Service, and deleted as soon as you delete your account or the workspace;
  • Issue reports: kept as needed after the issue is resolved, deleted along with your account, and you can ask us to delete them at any time;
  • Request logs, usage statistics, traces and station run logs: kept for the retention periods of Cloudflare, PostHog and Axiom respectively, then deleted automatically.

Where laws or regulations require a longer retention period, that period applies.

10. Your rights

You have the right to access, copy, correct, supplement and delete your personal information, to ask us to explain our rules for processing personal information, and to withdraw consent and delete your account.

  • Workspace owners can delete a workspace in the app;
  • Deleting your account: choose “Delete account” in the app's account settings. Your account information, sign-in sessions, push tokens and issue reports are deleted at once; workspaces with only you in them are deleted too; you're removed from the other workspaces you're in. If you're the only owner of a workspace that has other members, first make someone else an owner or delete that workspace. Deletion can't be undone;
  • Turning off usage statistics: turn off “Send usage and error reports” in the app's settings;
  • For other requests, also email hi@still.fail. We'll reply within 15 working days.

Your chat history and files are on your stations, where you can view, export and delete them directly.

11. Security

Communication between devices and stations is end-to-end encrypted, the website uses HTTPS throughout, only hashes of sign-in credentials are stored, and access to servers is tightly restricted. But no system is perfectly secure; if a personal information security incident occurs, we'll notify you and take remedial measures as required by law. If you find a security vulnerability, please report it privately as described in SECURITY.md.

Agents running on a station have the permissions of the corresponding system account on that machine. Run stations on machines and accounts you trust.

12. Minors

The Service is for users aged 16 and over. If you're under 16, please don't use the Service. If we find that we've collected personal information from a child under 14 without a guardian's consent, we'll delete it as soon as possible.

13. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy. Updates will be posted on this page with a revised effective date. We'll tell you in advance, in the app or by email, about material changes (such as adding a new third-party service provider or changing the purposes of use).
